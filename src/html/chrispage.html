Problems with Traditional Energy Sources

There are many problems with traditional sources of Energy. meaning they come from finite natural resources that cannot be replenished within a human lifetime. Once these supplies are extracted, they are gone.
What Are Tradidional Energy Sources?
Traditional energy sources are energy sources that are used, both now and in the past, to generate energy. However, most of these energy sources are non renewable, meaning that they cannot be replenished within a human lifetime. This means that we are using up the sources faster than they can be regenerated, and will run out. This is a huge problem for humanity, as we rely on energy for almost everything. Along with the limited supply of these sourecs, traditional energy sources have a huge impact on the environment, and are the leading contributor to climate change. A prime example of this is the burning of fossil fuels, which release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. This has a knock on affect on the global temprature, leading to rising sea levels, a rise in the rate of extreme weather events, and a loss of biodiversity. This is a huge problem for our planet, and is something that must be addressed.

Examples of non renewable energy include:

Source About the Source Common Applications
Coal A dark sedimentary rock, formed from old plant matter, compressed, and heated deep inside earth for millions of years Power Plants, Old Locomotives
Petroleum An oily and flammable liquid, mostly made from hydrocarbons (hydrogen and carbon atoms), found deep in the earth Transportation Fuel, Electricity and Heating, Creating Plastics and other materials.
Natural Gas Formed from the remains of plants and animals, the creation of natural gas takes millions of years. It is mostly made up of Methane (CH4), and other Hydrocarbons, along with trace amounts of other gases, such as Carbon Dioxide, Nitrogen, and others. Usualy used for electicity generation, heating, manufacturing, and transport

How long until we run out?

Of course, we have know definite answer to this question. It all comes down to what sources we look at, and the consumtion rate. However, estimates show that there may not be much time left with these sources. For coal, we're estimated to have about 30 to 55 years left, lasting us until a time period between the 2050s to the 2080s. For petroleum, theres an estimated 40 to 53 years left, with it expected to run out in the 60s. For Natural Gases, its believed to be able to last another 70 to 130 years, with it being estimated to run out between the late 2090s to the 2100s.

Issues from using traditional energy sources

Traditional energy sources have several downsides and impacts, spanning from environmental impacts, to being at the heart of geopolitical issues. Firstly, the cost of some non renewable energy sources can be high, impacting the economy and the cost of living crisis. This means some people cannot make trips and journeys, and are limited to only nessicary travel, such as commuting to work Additionally, the lack of availability of these sources can lead to tensions rising between countries that have access to the resources, and those that dont. This can lead to conflicts and wars, as countries battle for access to these resources.
