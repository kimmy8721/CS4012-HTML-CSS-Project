Problems with Traditional Energy Sources

There are many problems with traditional sources of Energy. meaning they come from finite natural resources that cannot be replenished within a human lifetime. Once these supplies are extracted, they are gone.

Examples of non renewable energy include:

Source About the Source Common Applications
Coal A dark sedimentary rock, formed from old plant matter, compressed, and heated deep inside earth for millions of years Power Plants, Old Locomotives
Petroleum An oily and flammable liquid, mostly made from hydrocarbons (hydrogen and carbon atoms), found deep in the earth Transportation Fuel, Electricity and Heating, Creating Plastics and other materials.
Natural Gas Formed from the remains of plants and animals, the creation of natural gas takes millions of years. It is mostly made up of Methane (CH4), and other Hydrocarbons, along with trace amounts of other gases, such as Carbon Dioxide, Nitrogen, and others. Usualy used for electicity generation, heating, manufacturing, and transport

How long until we run out?

Of course, we have know definite answer to this question. It all comes down to what sources we look at, and the consumtion rate. However, estimates show that there may not be much time left with these sources. For coal, we're estimated to have about 30 to 55 years left, lasting us until a time period between the 2050s to the 2080s. For petroleum, theres an estimated 40 to 53 years left, with it expected to run out in the 60s. For Natural Gases, its believed to be able to last another 70 to 130 years, with it being estimated to run out between the late 2090s to the 2100s.

Problems emerging from the use of non-renewable energy sources

The use of non renewable energy sources creates many problems. These go beyond the immediate environmental impacs, including geopolitical issues, economic issues, and social issues. For example, the cost of living is often being negatively impacted by the use of no renewable energy sources. For example, the cost of petrol and diesel is going up due to the limited supply of the resources. The lack of resources is also causing geopolitical issues. Tensions are rising betewwn countries that have access to the resources, and countries that dont. This is causing conflicts and wars to grow. Due to the cost, people cant afford to travel, and are forced to stay home, and have to restrict their travel to only essential trips, such as going to work, or to a shop. This is causing social issues, as people are unable to see their friends and family, and are forced to stay home.
